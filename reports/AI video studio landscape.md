# Project V's stack already matches the field, and these tests could improve it

Project V already sits close to the best-evidenced open approach. Nobody found in this research sells or publishes a finished, fully moving, 4K, long-form film with narration, music, edit and publishing kit made from one prompt. Commercial platforms sell clips by the second or credit, or they assemble stock footage and avatars. Open-source "idea to film" projects (ViMax is the only mature one) glue together paid APIs and have no durable job engine, no 4K and no cost tracking. So Project V's orchestration layer is the real differentiator, and the stack the owner already chose (Wan 2.2 14B fp8 with LightX2V and SVI, FLUX.2 klein 4B keyframes, ElevenLabs, ffmpeg, RunPod pods with a watchdog) survives every check in this report. It is licence-clean for a monetised channel (Apache 2.0 or MIT for the video and image models), and it is far cheaper per second than hosted models (about $0.05-0.60 per second hosted against roughly cents per second self-hosted, an inference from rented-GPU rates). Five findings should change what the owner does next. First, **Veo 3.1 through the Gemini API is gone** (shut down 22 Oct 2026), and Sora's API is gone, so plans that assumed either are stale. Second, **YouTube's new Partner Programme bar from 1 Feb 2027 is official**: 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views for new channels. That favours long-form over Shorts for a new channel. Third, **several top-ranked "open" models cannot be used on a global channel** because their licences exclude the US, EU, UK and Korea (MiniMax H3, Pika H3 Flex) or the EU, UK and Korea (Tencent Hunyuan). Fourth, **the quality rankings conflict** (Wan 3.0 is first on one leaderboard and thirteenth on another), so no single "best model" claim can be trusted. Fifth, the cheapest real upgrades are tests costing about Rs 100-1,500 each: SeedVR2 against Real-ESRGAN, a hosted "hero shot" tier, LongCat-Video and LTX-2.5 A/Bs, and a better quality gate. The full adoption plan is in the last sections.

## How to read this report

All research notes are dated 24 Nov 2026 unless stated. Rupee figures use Rs 94 per US dollar, and where the notes did not give a rupee figure, the conversion is my arithmetic. Three labels are used throughout. **Verified** means a vendor's own page, a model card, a GitHub API response or an official YouTube page was read. **Reported** means credible secondary coverage, or an aggregator or reseller price. **Unverified** means a single weak source, a vendor's own marketing claim, or a number the research could not read. Where two sources disagree, both numbers are shown and flagged "conflict". A few terms, defined once: a *keyframe* is a still picture that anchors a shot; *image-to-video (I2V)* animates a keyframe; *fp8* is a smaller number format that makes models fit on cheaper GPUs; *Elo* is a ranking score built from blind votes (higher is better); *LUFS* is a loudness unit; *Content ID* is YouTube's automatic music-matching system.

One limit applies to the whole report. The research was done with search tools that were rate-limited, and the page-reading tool summarises pages rather than quoting them. Many commercial products (Canva, CapCut, Leonardo, LTX Studio pricing, Captions, Visla) could not be read at all. They are listed as unverified and are not guessed.

## Stage 1 and 2: ideas, scripts, planning and quality gates

### What the field does

Open research systems split planning into "role agents". MovieAgent (NUS, 2025) uses a Director agent that cuts a synopsis into sub-scripts, a Scene Plan agent that records characters, tone, style and cinematography, and a Shot Plan agent that records shot type, camera movement, who is in shot and subtitles. It keeps a "character bank" of names, portraits and optional voice samples ([arXiv 2503.07314](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07314v1)). ViMax (HKUDS, MIT, 13,020 stars) presents itself as director, screenwriter, producer and video generator in one. Its workflows are Idea2Video, Script2Video, Novel2Video and AutoCameo ([ViMax README](https://github.com/HKUDS/ViMax)). Its technical report describes a "dependency-aware" mechanism that tracks character and environment state across scene boundaries ([arXiv 2606.07649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07649)). The commercial equivalent is LTX Studio's storyboard: it splits a script into scenes and shots, creates a still per shot, and maps characters and locations to reusable "Elements" built from 5-12 reference images, but these are vendor claims with no independent consistency test found ([LTX guide](https://ltx.studio/blog/how-to-create-a-consistent-character)). InVideo sells an agent layer ("Agent Two") over its credits, but the notes did not test its output quality ([InVideo pricing](https://invideo.io/pricing)).

The shared pattern is idea, synopsis, scenes, shots (each with type, camera move, characters and location), then a keyframe per shot, then video. Consistency is enforced at generation time, with reference images and a vision-language model (VLM, an AI that can look at pictures) picking the better of several candidate keyframes. ViMax generates two keyframe candidates and lets a vision model choose, at higher API cost, and its own README says so. DramaAgent (an AACL 2026 paper) goes furthest on gating: three candidates per scene scored on identity consistency (0.35), semantic fidelity (0.30), temporal continuity (0.20) and audiovisual match (0.15), accepted at 0.80, with up to two repair rounds aimed at the weakest dimension. But its repo has 6 commits, 4 stars, no visible licence and was "checked with mock providers", so take the idea, not the code ([DramaAgent](https://github.com/AIGeeksGroup/DramaAgent)).

Script models are cheap. Verified list prices per million tokens (input / output): GPT-5.4 mini $0.75 / $4.50, GPT-5.4 $2.50 / $15, Claude Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10, Claude Opus 5.5 $4 / $20, Gemini 3.1 Pro $2 / $12 ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing), [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [Gemini](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). On an assumed 30,000 input and 15,000 output tokens per 10-minute script (my assumption, not measured), a GPT-5.4 mini pass is about $0.09 (Rs 8.5), Sonnet 5.5 about $0.21 (Rs 20) and an Opus 5.5 Director pass about $0.42 (Rs 40). Even five iterations stay under Rs 200. The notes found no current independent ranking of models for creative writing (the EQ-Bench rows did not load), so the owner's own blind A/B (he chose OpenAI gpt-5.4-mini scripts over Claude on 23 Nov 2026) remains the best evidence.

Fact-checking has an established recipe. SAFE (Google DeepMind) splits a long answer into single facts and searches the web for each. It agreed with human raters on about 72% of roughly 16,000 facts, and was right in 76% of a 100-case sample of disagreements, at over 20 times lower cost than human raters ([arXiv 2403.18802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)). VERISCORE extracts only checkable claims from text that mixes fact and opinion ([arXiv 2406.19276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19276)). The 72% figure means such a checker is a filter, not a guarantee, and most test data are Wikipedia-style facts, not science claims. Search costs are small: Anthropic's web search is $10 per 1,000 searches and Google's grounding is $14 per 1,000 after 5,000 free per month, so a script with 40-80 claims costs well under a few dollars ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [Gemini](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)).

Automatic video quality gates are the thinnest part of the field. VBench-2.0 scores five dimensions (human fidelity, controllability, creativity, physics, commonsense) with VLMs plus specialist detectors, and uses redundant questioning to limit judge hallucination ([arXiv 2503.21755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21755)). The older VBench adds subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness and "dynamic degree" ([arXiv 2311.17982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17982)). One 2026 paper describes VBench-2.0 as using a single LLaVA-Video-7B for yes/no questions, which conflicts with the original paper's emphasis on specialists ([arXiv 2605.03475](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.03475v1)). No source measured how accurately a Sonnet-class model spots video artefacts.

My recommended design for the gate (inference, not sourced): a plan-stage check before any GPU spend (schema valid, narration length matches shot durations, every shot references a real character and location, every claim verified); free signal checks after generation (black or frozen frames, silence, loudness, resolution); then a VLM judge on three to six sampled frames per clip using specific yes/no questions asked two ways, calibrated against the owner's own accept and reject history so thresholds follow his taste, not a benchmark.

### Retention structure is folklore until Project V measures its own

YouTube's own page confirms only mechanics: "Intro" is the share still watching after 30 seconds, spikes mean rewatching, dips mean abandonment, and a high Intro suggests the opening matched the title and thumbnail ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)). Numeric benchmarks ("70% at 30 seconds") come from creator-tool blogs and conflict with each other ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)). Vendor data (Metricool, 799,718 videos) says Shorts views grew 127% but with about three times less time per view, while estimated long-form ad revenue fell about 55% ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-youtube-study-2026/)). That is directional only, because it comes from a vendor. The practical advice is to have the script stage emit per-beat timings so the first 30 seconds can be checked against the title promise, treat "open loop every 60-90 seconds" as a hypothesis, and read each upload's retention curve rather than guru rules.

LightX2V's 4-step distillation could cut that several-fold, but nobody has measured it here.

### Availability and ranking conflicts, stated plainly

**Veo.** The Gemini API video documentation says the Veo 3.1 Preview models were shut down on 22 Oct 2026 and replaced by Gemini Omni Flash, with GA Veo available through Google's enterprise platform (the Vertex rebrand) ([Gemini API video docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/video)). Yet fal's pricing page, read the same day, still lists Veo 3.1 Fast at $0.15 per second ([fal pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing)), and several aggregator blogs list Veo 3.1 prices dated August or September 2026 ([benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/media-pricing/veo)). These do not necessarily contradict each other (a reseller may route to the enterprise platform), but the official Veo per-second price on the enterprise platform was never read. The Veo price table above is therefore **reported, not verified**. Veo 3.1 4K at 4 seconds costs 2,080 credits on Magnific, a reseller ([Magnific](https://www.magnific.com/pricing)).

**Sora.** OpenAI's own deprecations page says the Videos API and the sora-2 models shut down on 24 Sep 2026 with no one-to-one replacement ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). The consumer app closing on 26 Apr 2026 is secondary reporting only. One FAQ that said the API ended in April contradicts OpenAI and should be disregarded.

**Rankings.** Artificial Analysis (AA) is a blind-vote arena, and its boards disagree across snapshots. One text-to-video-with-audio board (read 24 Nov) puts Wan 3.0 first at Elo 1175, ahead of Utopai X (1173), MiniMax H3 (1163) and Seedance 2.5 (1155), with Veo and Kling absent from the visible top 15 ([AA text-to-video](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). The image-to-video board (also 24 Nov) puts Seedance 2.5 first (1161), Utopai X second, Boreal-H3 third, and **Wan 3.0 only thirteenth (1112)** ([AA image-to-video](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). Earlier snapshots had Seedance 2.0 at 1211 or Gemini Omni Flash at 1245. The scales and orders move quickly, and the pages carry no as-of date. Project V works image-to-video (it animates keyframes), so the image-to-video board is the more relevant one, but it measures taste, not 4K sharpness. The notes also list "Hyperflow H3" on AA while a search for it found nothing, so its existence is unverified. Wan 2.2, Project V's actual model, does not appear on AA's open-weights board at all, so it has no Elo to compare. The owner's own A/B judgments (16 fps Wan looked fake; last-frame chaining beat first-last) remain the best quality evidence for this stack.

### Keyframes

Keyframes are never the cost problem. Seedream 4.5 is $0.04 per image on OpenRouter and fal; FLUX.2 klein 4B is about $0.014 hosted (search snippet, vendor page unread); Nano Banana Pro is $0.13-0.15 at 1K-2K and $0.24-0.30 at 4K ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/bytedance-seed/seedream-4.5/pricing), [WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/posts/seedream-5-0-vs-nano-banana-pro-gpt-image-flux-klein-qwen-image-comparison-2026)). A film with 60 keyframes costs about $0.9 (Rs 85) on klein, $2.4 (Rs 225) on Seedream 4.5 and $9 (Rs 850) on Nano Banana Pro at 2K (my arithmetic). One licence trap: **FLUX.2 klein 4B is Apache 2.0, but klein 9B is non-commercial** ([HF klein-4B](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B), [HF klein-9B](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-9B)). Project V must stay on 4B. The notes also flag that Seedream 4.5 and 5.0 may already supersede the Seedream version in the stack.

### Camera control and consistency

Wan2.2-Fun-Camera-Control (Alibaba PAI) adds preset pans and zooms and has ComfyUI documentation, with a 4-step Lightning LoRA workflow and 14B fp8 using about 84% VRAM at 640x640 on a 4090D ([ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/wan/wan2-2-fun-camera)). Its model card lists Apache 2.0 for the Control variant; the Camera variant's licence rests on a search summary and needs a check ([HF Fun-A14B-Control](https://huggingface.co/alibaba-pai/Wan2.2-Fun-A14B-Control)). It is likely a separate 14B checkpoint, not a LoRA overlay (my inference). SVI (MIT, ICLR 2026 Oral) fine-tunes Wan 2.2 with "error recycling" so clips chain without drifting, and the official ComfyUI workflow takes a separate prompt per clip ([SVI](https://github.com/vita-epfl/Stable-Video-Infinity)). Community reports say the camera is hard to move with SVI and that 3 overlap frames work best ([SVI project page](https://wanx-troopers.github.io/svi.html)). No public test combines Fun Camera, LightX2V and SVI. For consistency across shots, open workflows use reference keyframes (Qwen-Image-Edit 2509 or 2511) and VACE or Animate rather than one model ([RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-workflows/create-coherent-scenes-qwen-image-edit-wan-2-2-in-comfyui-cinematic-coherence-workflow)). Kijai's WanVideoWrapper (Apache 2.0) offers the widest feature set but calls itself a work-in-progress sandbox and does not list SVI, so pin a commit and use the official SVI workflow ([README](https://github.com/kijai/ComfyUI-WanVideoWrapper)). SVI's install pins an older Python and flash-attn stack, which can clash with Project V's newer torch, so it should run through the ComfyUI workflow in its own pinned image.

## Stage 4: audio (voice, music, effects, mixing)

### Narration

On Artificial Analysis's speech arena (25 Sep 2026, reported via an aggregator), Cartesia Sonic 3.6 was first at Elo 1279 ($49 per million characters), Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS second (1265, $16.49), and ElevenLabs v3 seventeenth (1169, $100). Eleven v4, launched 28 Sep 2026, is reported to have taken first place at 1319 and $80 per million characters ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/best-text-to-speech-models-september-2026-ranked-priced), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/elevenlabs-new-v4-speech-model-supports-more-expression-control-and-90-languages/)). The v4 first-place claim comes through an aggregator plus a weak mention and must be checked on the live board ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)).

ElevenLabs list API prices (verified, vendor page) are $0.08 per 1,000 characters for v3 and Multilingual v2 and $0.04 for Flash and Turbo, with Music at $0.15 per minute and sound effects at $0.12 per minute ([ElevenLabs API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). At about 750-1,000 characters per spoken minute, narration costs roughly $0.06-0.08 per minute (Rs 5.6-7.5), so a 10-minute narration is about $0.6-0.8 (Rs 56-75) at list price. Cheaper rivals: Gemini TTS about $0.0135 per minute now (promo; about $0.027 from 1 Jan 2027) and Cartesia about $0.038 per minute (derived). On the monthly plans, the notes conflict: the consumer pricing page lists Starter at 30,000 credits, Creator 121,000, Pro 600,000 and Scale 1.8 million, while the API page shows different per-plan allowances; one page says API access needs Pro or above ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing), [API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). Plans (monthly): Free $0, Starter $6, Creator $22 (about Rs 2,070), Pro $99 (Rs 9,300), Scale $299, Business $990, with UPI payment available in India. The free plan has no commercial rights; the Starter-and-up commercial licence is stated on the vendor page for Music, and third-party guides say the same for voice, but the official page read gave no explicit voice-licence text. So the owner should read the Terms page before publishing and confirm that rights attach at the time a file is generated.

Open fallbacks rank far lower: Kokoro (Apache 2.0, 82M parameters, Elo about 1063) and Chatterbox (MIT, Elo about 1023). Chatterbox's own README now says it has been "retired research and is no longer maintained" as of August 2026, and Resemble AI no longer sells TTS ([Chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox)). Treat it as a frozen offline fallback with no bug fixes. Fish S2 Pro needs a paid commercial licence, VibeVoice's card says research use only, and Voxtral TTS is CC BY-NC ([HF s2-pro](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro), [HF VibeVoice](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/VibeVoice-1.5B)). Because the owner is unhappy with the current voice, the contenders for a blind A/B are Eleven v4, Cartesia Sonic 3.6 and Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS. He asked on 18 Nov to pause voice tests, so this needs his go-ahead; the cost is under Rs 100 for three short samples.

On Shorts, a clip over 60 seconds with any active copyright claim is blocked globally, whereas under 60 seconds it is not subject to that block ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877)). Mitigation: keep a provenance row for every track, upload Private first, avoid prompts that name artists, and keep a dispute route ready.

### Sound effects and native audio

ElevenLabs SFX ($0.12 per minute, described as royalty-free) is the safe default ([API pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)). MMAudio's code is MIT but its weights are CC-BY-NC, so it cannot be used for monetised publishing ([MMAudio](https://github.com/hkchengrex/MMAudio)). HunyuanVideo-Foley needs 16-20 GB (8-12 GB offloaded) and carries the Tencent territory licence tag, with the licence text itself unread ([HF](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-Foley)). Earshot (MIT, CPU only, 922 designed sounds rendered from code, free for commercial use) suits whooshes, hits and interface sounds but not realistic foley ([earshot](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/earshot)); the notes also found an unrelated speech-to-text app with the same name, so confirm the right repo. Native-audio video models (Veo, Sora 2, LTX-2.x, Wan 2.5+, newer Kling) would bypass this stage, but open Wan 2.2 is silent, so Project V's separate audio stage is correct for its stack. Reviews conflict over whether Veo or Sora audio sounds more natural, and the tests were small ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/ai-image-video/sora-2-vs-veo-3-1-i-tested-both-ai-video-generators-with-7-audio-prompts-heres-the-winner)). Lip-sync is not needed for narrated documentaries; if ever required, Sync Labs costs about $0.04-0.08 per second and LatentSync is self-hostable, but their licences are unverified ([DEV guide](https://dev.to/jondavis/lip-sync-tools-in-2026-a-developers-buyers-guide-with-benchmarks-trade-offs-4e4h)).

### Mixing

YouTube normalises to about -14 LUFS integrated with true peak at or below -1 dBTP (third-party guides; one cites -16, which conflicts) ([Sweetwater](https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/how-to-master-audio-for-youtube/), [Frame.io](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/loudness-for-youtube)). ffmpeg's `loudnorm` works best in two passes ([DEV guide](https://dev.to/javidjamae/ffmpeg-loudnorm-filter-ebu-r128-loudness-normalization-guide-15d4)). My suggested recipe, from general practice and not a fetched source: separate stems for narration, music and effects; music about 12-18 dB below the voice with `sidechaincompress` keyed by the narration; final two-pass loudnorm to I=-14, TP=-1.5; and an automated loudness test that fails the build on regression. Verify the -14 figure on YouTube's help page before hard-coding.

## Stage 5: upscaling and frame interpolation

SeedVR2 (ByteDance, Apache 2.0 stated for SeedVR and SeedVR2) is a video-native diffusion upscaler and the strongest open alternative to Topaz in the notes ([HF SeedVR2-7B](https://huggingface.co/ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR2-7B)). Real-ESRGAN works frame by frame, so invented detail changes between frames and can shimmer, which is the main quality risk in Project V's current upscale stage. ComfyUI's docs time SeedVR2 at about 312 s for a 10-second 1080p clip on a local 5090, against about 374 s for Topaz Astra ([ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/utility/video-upscale), read as a search summary). One blogger found SeedVR2 sharper and more natural than Topaz, but on a handful of clips, not timed benchmarks ([WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/posts/seedvr2-vs-topaz)). An older figure had a 4090 taking 783 s for 149 frames from 480p to 1080p on the 3B fp8 model ([InstaSD](https://www.instasd.com/post/seedvr2-video-upscale-comfyui-topaz-alternative)), which predates later optimisation and is not a 4K figure. Nobody has timed SeedVR2 at 1080p to 4K on Wan footage on a 4090, so that is the first thing to measure. SeedVR2 needs a rented GPU; the 4 GB laptop cannot run it.

Hosted prices: fal charges $0.001 per output megapixel for SeedVR2, so 4K (8.29 megapixels) at 16 fps for one minute (960 frames) is about **$8 per minute (Rs 750)**, tripling to about $24 if upscaling happens after interpolating to 48 fps (my arithmetic from the fal formula, itself a search summary). Topaz on fal is $0.01/s up to 720p, $0.02/s to 1080p and $0.08/s above, about $4.80 per minute at 4K, but it is a closed model ([fal Topaz](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/topaz/upscale/video/llms.txt)). Topaz's own price is in conflict: sources report the perpetual licence ended in Oct 2025, with subscriptions at about $299 per year or $49.99 per month on one listing, while one site claims a $299 lifetime licence ([Activepieces](https://www.activepieces.com/blog/topaz-video-ai-pricing-2026-full-plans-and-real-costs), [WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/posts/seedvr2-vs-topaz)). Topaz's own page was not read.

For interpolation, RIFE remains the fast cheap default; the ncnn port is MIT-licensed, and Practical-RIFE says versions 4.24 and later suit diffusion-model video ([rife-ncnn-vulkan](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fnihui%2Frife-ncnn-vulkan), [Practical-RIFE](https://huggingface.co/vidfom/LTX-2-S/blob/main/Practical-RIFE/README.md)). GIMM-VFI models motion as a continuous function of time and handles fast motion where RIFE ghosts, but is several times slower, according to community notes ([GIMM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08680v5), [comfy.icu](https://comfy.icu/node/GIMMVFI_interpolate)). 16 to 48 fps is an exact 3 times, which suits RIFE; 16 to 60 is not an integer and favours GIMM-VFI. No head-to-head on Wan output exists, and the best order (interpolate then upscale, or the reverse) needs a local test, judged by the owner's eye on fast limbs and camera pans.

## Stage 6: editing, captions and motion graphics

Hosted JSON-to-video editors are poor value at 4K. Shotstack is quoted at about $0.20-0.40 per minute, JSON2Video about $0.20-0.25 per minute and Creatomate about $1.93 per minute at 4K, all from competitor or review blogs and so unverified ([Opus blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/best-video-editing-apis-for-developers), [WireFlow](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/creatomate-pricing)). Remotion Lambda compute starts at about $0.013 per minute but the licence needs a paid company licence above small-team thresholds ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/lambda)). HyperFrames (HeyGen's HTML-to-video, Apache 2.0) is the licence-clean alternative, though it is weak at distributed rendering and its details come from review summaries ([GitHub](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)). Project V's ffmpeg editor and OTIO-to-FCP7 XML route needs no vendor and no per-minute fee, so keep it. The notes did not research colour grading, stabilisation, OTIO fidelity into Premiere, or Manim (a free maths-animation library) in any depth, so diagrams and lower thirds remain a low-confidence "render to transparent layers locally and composite with ffmpeg" suggestion.

For captions, WhisperX adds wav2vec2 forced alignment to faster-whisper for per-word timestamps; it improves timing, not text accuracy ([DEV guide](https://dev.to/dibi8/whisperx-22k-stars-production-asr-setup-guide-2026-3o13)). Because Project V already holds the script and generates the narration, aligning the known script (the alignment step alone) should give accurate word timings with no transcription errors. That is my inference, not a sourced finding. Hosted Whisper is about $0.006 per minute (Groq quotes conflict at $0.02-0.04 per hour), so local transcription on the pod or CPU is effectively free ([DEV comparison](https://dev.to/pietrus914/speech-to-text-api-comparison-whisper-api-options-in-2026-400h)). Commercial caption and clip tools are not generators: Submagic (Business: 4K 60 fps export, API at Rs 8.68-13 per minute), OpusClip (Pro $29 per month includes the API), Vizard and Descript (Creator $24-35 per month, 4K export) ([Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing), [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/pricing), [Descript](https://www.descript.com/pricing)). Styled captions are a rendering task Project V can do itself from word timings. OpusClip's API is the only thing worth considering, and only for clipping long videos into Shorts, which Project V's own Shorts branch already covers.

### Serverless against pods, cold starts and owning hardware

A 20-minute job on a warm 4090 costs about $0.11 (Rs 10.7) on a Community pod, $0.30 on a Secure pod and $0.45 (Rs 42) on serverless plus billed model-load time (my arithmetic). Serverless wins only when a pod would sit idle about 75% of its paid time. It also caches only **one model per endpoint**, a poor fit for a pipeline that needs two Wan experts, a text encoder and a VAE ([RunPod model caching](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/endpoints/model-caching)). FlashBoot claims conflict (under 200 ms in one RunPod blog, "500 ms, P90 under 2 s" in another) and are for lightweight workers, not 40-80 GB diffusion stacks ([RunPod blog](https://www.runpod.io/blog/serverless-gpu-cold-starts-flashboot)). Documented pod problems are image-pull stalls on cold hosts, stopped pods that come back with zero GPUs (network volumes are the recommended prevention), and capacity gaps ([RunPod zero-gpus](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/troubleshooting/zero-gpus)). Project V's own measured warm-start result (8-18 minutes with a warm image on RunPod and Vast, against 29 without) fits this. On Vast, destroy instances instead of stopping them: storage keeps billing while stopped, and a zero balance destroys instances and data ([markaicode](https://markaicode.com/pricing/vast-ai-pricing/), third party).

Buying a 32 GB GPU does not pay back. An RTX 5090 in India was reported at Rs 4.3-5.7 lakh in August 2026 (sources conflict, November price unverified), which equals about 3,400-6,900 rented hours at Secure or Community rates, or 2.3-4.7 years at four GPU-hours a day, excluding the PC and power ([gpuprix](https://gpuprix.com/india/gpus/geforce-rtx-5090), [getpc.co.in](https://getpc.co.in/parts/gpu/rtx-5090-32gb)). The 4 GB laptop should stay for orchestration, editing and small tests.

### Orchestration

The documented pattern is a lease with a hard ceiling and an independent monitor: for example stop after 30 minutes idle, an 8-hour maximum, and a lease-expiry check run from outside the instance ([soong cost controls](https://axiomantic.github.io/soong/latest/architecture/cost-controls/)). Idle watchdogs keyed only to low GPU use can kill busy jobs that are CPU- or network-bound, so tie them to queue depth and in-flight jobs ([geekchamp](https://geekchamp.com/how-to-reduce-gpu-cloud-costs-when-ai-jobs-sit-idle/)). Project V already has the lease idea, and the notes recommend three additions: an external killer on a non-GPU host that calls the RunPod API, a per-job wall-clock cap, and tags that flag pods without a lease. Comparisons with Prefect, Dagster, Ray, ComfyDeploy and similar were not researched, and no source showed how named video start-ups structure their queues. Temporal with idempotent asset-ID activities matches the "checkpoint for safe retries" advice in the sources ([RunPod guide](https://www.runpod.io/articles/guides/reduce-cloud-gpu-expenses-without-sacrificing-performance)).

On speed, the evidence is mixed. LightX2V's 4-step Wan 2.2 distills have fp8 files of about 15 GB, and its vendor claim is about twice as fast as ComfyUI. SageAttention is reported as about 2 times faster than FlashAttention-2 at kernel level ([arXiv 2410.02367](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02367)). TurboDiffusion claims 38 s for a Wan 2.2 720p clip on a 5090, but a GitHub user measured 78-83 s ([issue #51](https://github.com/thu-ml/TurboDiffusion/issues/51)). Consistent with the owner's rule that savings ship off until an A/B shows equal quality, none of these should be turned on without a look at the output.

## Stage 10: YouTube policy, revenue and legal basics

### The monetisation bar and the policy buckets

The 1 Feb 2027 change is **confirmed by YouTube's own blog (10 Aug 2026) and help page**: new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus either 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, up from 4,000 hours or 10 million. The 500-subscriber tier (fan funding and shopping) stays at 3,000 hours or 3 million Shorts views. Existing partners are unaffected by the threshold but face a 10-million-view minimum for the Shorts Creator Pool and activity rules, and must accept updated terms in Studio by 31 Jan 2027 ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/), [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)). One earlier note called this unverified because a search summary disagreed with the official page then read; the later official reading settles it. The official page does not mention any India-specific threshold, so global rules apply (not verified for India). A brand-new channel will face the new bar. Shorts-only monetisation then needs about 10 million views per 90 days sustained, so long-form watch hours are the safer route.

On inauthentic content, YouTube's help page (renamed from "repetitious" on 15 Jul 2025) now has sections for Generic or Repetitive Content, Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content, and AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics. Not monetisable: image slideshows, templated storylines, the same character in the same situation with the same outcome, and stitching unrelated AI clips to shock. Allowed: recurring-character series where each video has its own storyline, the same intro and outro with different main content, and automation or AI-assisted scripts and visuals if the final product shows creative vision ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). The spam page names "the same background music and repetitive AI imagery with AI scripts" as an example of mass production ([YouTube Help 2801973](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973)). Enforcement is channel-wide. **Conflict:** secondary outlets date a "clarification" to 13-20 July 2026 and some call it a tightening, others (Tubefilter) say the rules are unchanged, and the official page shows no July 2026 revision note. A TechRepublic date mismatch was also flagged. So the three-bucket structure is official, but the July 2026 date is secondary only ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). A claim that January 2026 wiped 16 of the top-100 AI-slop channels (about 35 million subscribers combined) rests on a Kapwing study quoted by outlets, including an opinion piece by a commercial AI-film vendor ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm), [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/)). The same coverage says slop channels such as Bandar Apna Dost (2.07 billion views) were visible in 2025 ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/)).

### Disclosure, likeness and Shorts

Realistic AI content that could be mistaken for real people, places or events must be labelled in Studio. Clearly unrealistic or animated content, minor edits, and production help (scripts, thumbnails, upscaling, audio enhancement, cloning your own voice for voiceover) are exempt. Disclosure does not limit reach or monetisation, while repeated non-disclosure can lead to labels, removal or Partner Programme suspension. YouTube can also add labels itself using C2PA metadata, its own tools and detection ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). The safest default for photoreal output is to tick the disclosure on every upload (no monetisation penalty per the page). Likeness detection covers faces only; voice misuse goes through privacy complaints ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16440338)). Shorts can be up to three minutes if square or vertical ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877)).

### Revenue for an India-based channel

India-audience Shorts RPM is reported at about Rs 2-20 per 1,000 views (some sources up to Rs 40) and roughly 10% of long-form per view, from third-party sources only; YouTube publishes no figure ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-rpm-india-2026), [WsCube](https://www.wscubetech.com/blog/youtube-income-per-1000-views/)). A faceless trivia Shorts channel with 80,000 subscribers and 15 million monthly views reportedly earned about $1,100 per month, self-reported ([Korpi](https://korpi.ai/blog/how-much-do-faceless-youtube-channels-make)). At Rs 5-20 per 1,000 India-audience views, a Short costing under Rs 20 (the owner's target) breaks even only after roughly 1,000-4,000 views (inference), and an English-language, US- or UK-heavy audience matters more than volume. Without US tax information in AdSense, Google may withhold up to 24% of worldwide earnings (secondary source). Hosted video cannot meet the Rs 20 Short target: a 45-second Short on Veo 3.1 Lite at 720p is about $2.25 (Rs 211), which leaves self-hosted Wan with FLUX.2 klein keyframes as the only route. Public evidence on audience-growth tactics (loops, hooks, comment-steered storylines) is missing; the only support is Neural Viz's persistent universe, and the series allowance in the policy.

### Law

The US Copyright Office (29 Jan 2025) says human authorship is required, prompts alone are not enough and selecting among outputs generally is not, though human-authored elements and creative arrangement or modification can be protected ([Copyright Office](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)). India has no settled position, and litigation is pending ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/can-ai-artwork-get-copyright-delhi-high-court-orders-copyright-office-decision-on-stephen-thaler-plea-in-8-weeks)). Project V's human-authored script, edit and arrangement, backed by its provenance records, is its best evidence of authorship. Avoid other creators' characters; reusing IP (Dragon Ball-style channels) adds copyright and reused-content risk. None of this is legal advice.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which pipeline should Project V copy?" to "which gaps in a sound pipeline are worth money?". The field has no complete analogue, and the open projects that come closest depend on the same paid clip APIs that Project V avoids. That leaves Project V's real exposures in four narrow places: temporal flicker in the upscale stage, a thin automatic critic, an unresolved narration voice, and licence and policy hygiene (free-plan voices, "open" models with territorial exclusions, templated sameness across episodes). Each is cheaper to fix than a model swap, and the largest test budget in the plan is about Rs 1,400.

The more useful discovery is how fast the outside facts move. Veo's API route, Sora, the YouTube monetisation bar and the model rankings each changed within weeks, and two ranking boards read on the same day disagree about Wan 3.0 by twelve places. A quality-first studio should therefore keep its decisions anchored to its own blind A/B results, record every licence check in the provenance rows, and re-verify any price or model ID before spending, as its own rule 7 already says. The channel's best protection against both the policy and the market is the thing the research keeps pointing back to: a directed series where each episode has its own script, treatment and sound.
