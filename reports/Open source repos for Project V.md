# Ten open repos that raise Project V's quality

Project V gets the most from open source in four places, not by swapping its core stack. Those places are the automatic critic (VideoReward, VBench custom-input dimensions, keyframe scorers), timing and finishing (Qwen3-ForcedAligner, beat_this, SeedVR2, Practical-RIFE), keyframe consistency (Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 plus ai-toolkit LoRAs), and lossless speed-ups that are already half-wired (FA3 or SageAttention2++ plus diffusers regional compilation). For generation itself, Gemini and Wan 2.2 stay in place. The one open video model worth a structured A/B now is **LTX-2.5**, because its licence is the cleanest. **MiniMax H3** leads open-weights image-to-video on quality, but its licence is restrictive and it needs a lot of hardware. The Wan line has **no open weights after 2.2**, so "upgrade to Wan 2.7" is not a self-hosted option. Licences matter more than benchmarks for this owner. Many of the best-scoring open weights are non-commercial: FLUX.2 [dev], Qwen-Image-2.1, Fish S2 Pro, YuE2, MMAudio, DOVER/FAST-VQA/Q-Align and madmom's models. A YouTube channel that may be monetised makes those risky. Before anything else, the owner should decide the monetisation question. That one decision settles about a dozen downstream choices. All findings are as of 17 Nov 2026. Several weight licences were read only from secondary sources; these are flagged below as needing a check of the primary LICENSE file.

## Lossless speed comes from attention and compilation, not from caching

The rest of the speed-up options are mostly lossy or need an engine swap. The cheapest remaining wins are inside diffusers. **FA3 (`_flash_3_hub`) computes exactly the same result**, and **SageAttention2++ (Apache-2.0) claims 2–5x attention speed-up "without losing end-to-end metrics"** on Hopper through its `sageattn_qk_int8_pv_fp8_cuda_sm90` kernel ([SageAttention](https://github.com/thu-ml/SageAttention)). `wan.py` already exposes both. Diffusers' **`compile_repeated_blocks()` gives speed similar to full compilation, with up to 8–10x shorter compile time** ([diffusers compile docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/optimization/fp16)). That matters on Modal, where containers start cold. Two practical points follow. The inductor cache should persist on the Modal volume. The lightx2v LoRAs need to be fused, or hot-swapped, before compiling, and fast-mode and hybrid-mode clips should keep fixed shapes so that switching between them does not trigger recompiles. That last point is my inference and has not been tested.

ModelTC/LightX2V (Apache-2.0, very active) is the engine behind the LoRAs Project V already uses. Its other gains are modest for a quality-first user. On **Wan 2.1** I2V at 720p on an H200, its bf16 path ran 1.30x faster than official and FP8 ran 1.45x faster. The 2.83x row depends on TeaCache at threshold 0.2, which is measurably lossy ([LightX2V benchmark](https://github.com/ModelTC/LightX2V/blob/main/docs/EN/source/getting_started/benchmark_source.md)). Adopting it means replacing the diffusers pipeline. Step caching has **no published evidence of working with a 4-step distilled LoRA**. With four steps there is almost nothing to skip, so caching is only relevant to the 12-step hybrid mode. Among the caches, **EasyCache published the best trade-off (2.54x on Wan2.1-1.3B at PSNR 25.24 / LPIPS 0.095, against TeaCache 2.00x at PSNR 22.57)** and lists Wan 2.2 I2V scripts ([EasyCache](https://github.com/H-EmbodVis/EasyCache)). **cache-dit** is diffusers-native and lists Wan 2.2 I2V, but publishes no Wan 2.2 numbers ([cache-dit](https://github.com/vipshop/cache-dit)). Sparse attention methods are lossy and lack Wan 2.2 I2V support: Radial Attention has a Wan 2.2 T2V script only ([Radial Attention](https://github.com/mit-han-lab/radial-attention)), and Sparse VideoGen covers Wan 2.1 only ([SVG](https://github.com/svg-project/Sparse-VideoGen)).

## Open challengers to Wan 2.2: MiniMax H3 leads, LTX-2.5 is the practical A/B

The Artificial Analysis open-weights image-to-video board shows a clear top tier as of 17 Nov 2026. **MiniMax H3 is at Elo 1134, Hyperflow H3 at 1119 and Pika H3 Flex at 1114, with LTX-2.5 far behind at 912** ([AA open-weights I2V](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video/open-weights)). Wan 2.2 is not on the board, so there is no direct number for it. The Wan-AI organisation's last open release is Wan2.2-Animate (Nov 2025). Weight claims for Wan 2.5/2.6/2.7 come from SEO sites and community re-uploads ([HowAIWorks](https://howaiworks.ai/blog/alibaba-wan-open-weights-stopped-at-2-2); [LocalAIMaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/wan-2-7-open-source)).

MiniMax H3 is a 33B model with a Qwen3-VL-32B text encoder. Its reference serving config uses four GPUs. Its open output is capped at **768p, 24 fps, 4–15 s with native stereo audio**, because the 2K upscaler is not open-sourced. It ships under the "MiniMax H3 Community License", with an application form for the USA/EU/UK/South Korea ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3)). Secondary sources say the licence also prohibits distillation ([Runpod](https://www.runpod.io/blog/minimax-h3-the-open-weight-omni-modal-video-model-and-what-it-takes-to-run-it)). LTX-2.5 is a 22B model with native audio. It accepts image conditioning at any frame index, supports 4K through its spatial upscaler, and is supported in diffusers and ComfyUI. Its commercial use is **free under US$10M annual revenue** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5)). That makes it the easiest model to benchmark against Wan 2.2 on Project V's own space shots. HunyuanVideo 1.5 (8.3B, 720p I2V plus an official 1080p SR stage) is cheap to run. Its Tencent licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea but not India ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-1.5)). SkyReels-V3's V2V 14B is the most direct open tool for **extending clips beyond 5 s** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Skywork/SkyReels-V3-V2V-14B)). I found no model card or licence for Hyperflow H3 or Pika H3 Flex, so they cannot be ranked responsibly yet.

## Finishing: SeedVR2 for 4K finals, RIFE for frame rate

**SeedVR2 (ByteDance-Seed, Apache-2.0, ICLR 2026) is the quality pick for upscaling.** It is a one-step restorer in 3B and 7B sizes. One H100-80G handles 100 frames at 720×1280, but 1080p/2K needs four H100s ([SeedVR](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR)). Going from 720p to 4K will therefore need chunking or tiling. **FlashVSR** (Apache-2.0 code; weight licence not stated) runs at about **17 fps at 768×1408 on an A100**. FlashVSR+ followed in Oct 2026 ([FlashVSR](https://github.com/OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR)). Community and vendor tests rate FlashVSR faster but lower in quality, with grid artifacts on close inspection ([moosky.ai](https://moosky.ai/blog/flash-vsr-vs-seedvr2)). It fits drafts and Shorts. Upscale-A-Video is reportedly non-commercial ([ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/upscale-a-video-for-creators-capabilities-limits-and-practical-alternatives/)). For 16→24 fps, **Practical-RIFE (MIT, including v4.25 weights)** is the fast default ([Practical-RIFE](https://github.com/hzwer/Pratical-RIFE); [HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/daydreamlive/RIFE/blob/main/README.md)). **GIMM-VFI** removes RIFE's ghosting on fast pans but is several times slower ([comfy.icu](https://comfy.icu/node/GIMMVFI_interpolate)). FILM (Apache-2.0) is the robust middle option ([comfyui-wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/es/models/frame-interpolation/frame-interpolation)). Any 24 fps-native challenger model removes the interpolation step entirely.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/SeedVR) (SeedVR2) | Apache-2.0 | Final 4K restoration | Highest; possible flicker on fine texture | Medium–high (chunking) |
| 2 | [OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR](https://github.com/OpenImagingLab/FlashVSR) | Apache-2.0 code; weights unstated | Fast 4x SR for drafts and Shorts | Good; grid artifacts | Medium (A100-tuned kernels) |
| 3 | [hzwer/Practical-RIFE](https://github.com/hzwer/Pratical-RIFE) | MIT | 16→24/30 fps | Good on slow shots | Low |
| 4 | GIMM-VFI | Not verified | Interpolation on fast motion | Best on pans | Medium |
| 5 | FILM | Apache-2.0 | Large-motion interpolation | Robust | Low |

## Keyframes and narration: Apache-licensed Qwen models carry consistency and alignment

The image models with the best scores are non-commercial. **Qwen-Image-2.1 (Elo 1,044) is research-only, and FLUX.2 [dev] (1,000) uses the FLUX Non-Commercial License** ([The Open Weights](https://theopenweights.com/leaderboards/text-to-image); [FLUX.2-dev card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-dev)). The FLUX.2 card's statement that outputs may be used commercially conflicts with its separate self-hosted commercial terms, so it is not a safe default. The coherent Apache-2.0 stack is **Qwen-Image-2512**, which "significantly reduces the 'AI-generated' look" ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2512)), plus **Qwen-Image-Edit-2511**, which offers better character consistency, multi-image input and built-in relighting and new-viewpoint LoRAs ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511)). For a house style, **ostris/ai-toolkit (MIT)** trains LoRAs for Qwen-Image/Edit, Z-Image base, FLUX.2 and Wan 2.2, and ships a Modal training guide ([ai-toolkit](https://github.com/ostris/ai-toolkit)). The same tool can carry a style into Wan clips; that is an inference, not yet tested. Z-Image Turbo/Base (Apache-2.0) stays the draft tier. FLUX.1-era identity adapters such as PuLID, UNO and USO matter only if real-person likeness becomes a need.

For narration, **no open model has been shown to beat Gemini TTS**, and the voices at the top of the leaderboards are non-commercial. Fish Audio S2 Pro (Elo 1108) uses a research licence, and VibeVoice is research-only; Microsoft removed its TTS code in Sep 2025 ([Cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/open-source-tts); [VibeVoice](https://github.com/microsoft/VibeVoice)). The commercially safe fallback is **Qwen3-TTS (Apache-2.0, more than 10 minutes of continuous speech, cloning from a few seconds of audio)**, but it has **no Hindi** ([arXiv 2601.15621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15621)). For Hindi, the candidates are NVIDIA Magpie TTS Multilingual (Elo 1064, NVIDIA Open Model Licence, commercial use allowed) ([Cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/open-source-tts)), AI4Bharat's IndicF5 and Indic Parler-TTS ([HF](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-parler-tts)). Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) is a cheap preview voice.

Caption timing has a clear winner, because Project V holds the exact script. **Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B (Apache-2.0) averages 42.9 ms alignment error, against 133.2 ms for WhisperX. On 300 s of audio the gap is 52.9 ms against 2,708 ms** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B)). Its limits are 5 minutes per call and no Hindi, so chunk at sentence boundaries and fall back to WhisperX or stable-ts with a Hindi wav2vec2 model for `hi`. MMS-FA weights are CC-BY-NC ([MMS-FA card](https://huggingface.co/chordai/mms-fa-aligner-coreml)).

## Audio, assembly and the critic: permissive tools fill the gaps Gemini leaves

**ACE-Step 1.5 is the strongest commercially clean music model**, released under MIT. It generates songs from 10 to 600 s and has had a 4B XL variant since Apr 2026. It includes a REST API server and LoRA training, and its authors claim quality "between Suno v4.5 and Suno v5" ([ACE-Step-1.5](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)). That claim is the authors' own, with no independent instrumental benchmark against Lyria. **YuE2** reaches near-parity with Suno v5 in blind tests, but its weights are **CC BY-NC 4.0**. Individual creators may sell outputs and companies need a licence ([Neurohive](https://neurohive.io/en/state-of-the-art/yue2-open-source-ai-song-generator/)). MusicGen weights are also CC-BY-NC ([audiocraft](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiocraft)). For sound effects, **HunyuanVideo-Foley** leads on its own benchmarks (Kling-Audio-Eval FD_PANNs 6.07 against MMAudio's 9.01). It uses the Tencent community licence ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-Foley)). MMAudio weights are CC-BY-NC ([MMAudio](https://github.com/hkchengrex/MMAudio)), and ThinkSound is research-only ([ThinkSound](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/ThinkSound)). Stable Audio Open is "better at sound effects and field recordings than at music" and uses the Stability Community License ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-audio-open-1.0)). For analysis, **beat_this releases both code and weights under MIT**, whereas madmom's model files are CC BY-NC-SA. That puts allin1, which depends on madmom, at risk as well ([beat_this](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this); [madmom](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom)). Demucs was archived on 1 Jan 2025. Use the adefossez fork or `demucs-infer` ([adefossez/demucs](https://github.com/adefossez/demucs)).

For assembly, **OpenTimelineIO plus otio-fcp-adapter (Apache-2.0)** stays the backbone. Its FCP7 XML export does **not carry transitions, effects or speed changes** ([otio-fcp-adapter](https://github.com/OpenTimelineIO/otio-fcp-adapter)). The Premiere export should therefore be a conform timeline with markers, and dissolves should be baked into the FFmpeg master. Burned-in ASS captions through libass need no extra licence. pycaps (MIT, alpha) suits styled Shorts captions ([pycaps](https://github.com/francozanardi/pycaps)). **Remotion is free only for companies of up to 3 people. Otherwise automated rendering costs US$0.01 per render with a US$100/month minimum, or US$25 per seat per month** ([Remotion pricing](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing.md)).

The critic is where open source adds the most. VLM judges like Gemini "consistently outperform or match specialized methods", but they are weakest on **flicker, motion naturalness and malformed bodies** ([UVE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09949v4)). **VideoReward/VideoAlign (MIT code, Qwen2-VL-2B) has separate motion-quality and visual-quality heads. It reaches 73.59% pairwise accuracy against human preference without ties, against 50.22% for VideoScore v1** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13918); [repo](https://github.com/KlingAIResearch/VideoAlign)). **VBench's custom-input dimensions (Apache-2.0)** give deterministic subject consistency, motion smoothness and dynamic degree. Dynamic degree directly catches the panned-still failure that rule 10 forbids ([VBench](https://github.com/Vchitect/VBench)). VBench needs CUDA ≤12.1 and detectron2, so it should run in its own container. VideoScore2 (MIT, active Sep 2026) adds written rationales suited to the Review UI ([VideoScore2](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/VideoScore2)). UVQ 1.5 (Apache-2.0) is a CPU gate faster than real time ([UVQ](https://github.com/google/uvq)). DOVER, FAST-VQA and Q-Align use the S-Lab licence, which permits use for "non-commercial purpose" only ([DOVER](https://github.com/VQAssessment/DOVER)). For keyframes, **HPSv3** (MIT code; weights possibly CC BY-NC-SA per a secondary source), **VQAScore via t2v_metrics (Apache-2.0)** and the LAION aesthetic predictor can rank a best-of-N batch inside the Z-Image app before any I2V spend ([HPSv3](https://github.com/MizzenAI/HPSv3); [t2v_metrics](https://github.com/linzhiqiu/t2v_metrics)). Any threshold should be calibrated on 50–100 owner-rated shots before it auto-rejects anything.

## Licence decisions only the owner can make

Every licence question here goes back to one choice: **will the channel be monetised?** If it will, published output is commercial use. Non-commercial weights then become internal A/B baselines only. These are FLUX.2 [dev], Qwen-Image-2.1, Fish Audio S2 Pro, VibeVoice, F5-TTS, XTTS v2, MMS-FA, YuE2 (unless the owner qualifies as an "individual creator"), MusicGen, MMAudio, ThinkSound/PrismAudio, Upscale-A-Video, DOVER/FAST-VQA/Q-Align, madmom/allin1 model files, and possibly the HPSv3 weights. The remaining owner decisions follow:

| Decision | What the owner must accept or check |
|---|---|
| Tencent Hunyuan community licence (HunyuanVideo 1.5, HunyuanVideo-Foley, HunyuanImage 3.0) | Not open source. Excludes the EU/UK/South Korea (India is not excluded per secondary sources). Scale thresholds such as >100M MAU. Read the full text first-hand. |

## Conclusion

The research turns the "which model is best" question around. For Project V the binding constraints are licence terms and measurement, not model capability. The highest-leverage open-source additions are not generators. They are the instruments that tell the studio whether a shot is good (VideoReward, VBench dimensions, keyframe scorers) and the finishing tools that turn a good 720p, 16 fps clip into a 4K, 24 fps master (SeedVR2, RIFE, forced alignment, beat-aware assembly). These tools also make every later model A/B honest, including Wan 2.2 against LTX-2.5 and MiniMax H3. Without them, comparisons rest on leaderboard Elo measured on general content that is not documentary footage.

Two things are still unknown and should shape the next milestone rather than be assumed. First, no source measures any cache, fp8 path or sparse-attention method on Wan 2.2 I2V A14B with lightx2v LoRAs, so Project V's own `attention_ab.py` bench is the only valid evidence. Second, several weight licences (HPSv3, VideoReward checkpoints, FlashVSR, IndicF5, Kandinsky, SkyReels-V3) were not verified from primary files. A short licence-verification pass, run after the owner's monetisation decision, would turn most of the recommendations above into commitments.
