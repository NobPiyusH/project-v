# Wan 2.2 powers building blocks, not films

People use Alibaba's Wan models mainly as a controllable, self-hostable clip generator that they assemble into larger edits, and the open-weight line ends at Wan 2.2. The standard producer pipeline is a still keyframe, then Wan 2.2 image-to-video (I2V) with 4-step LightX2V speed LoRAs, then clip chaining, then a single late-stage upscale and frame interpolation, then a normal editor. Each job has its own variant: I2V/T2V for shots, first-last-frame for transitions, VACE for joining and inpainting, Animate for character swap, S2V or Wan-derived talking-head models for dialogue. Open Wan yields silent clips of about 5 s at 480p/720p and 16 fps; audio, 15 s clips and 1080p exist only in hosted Wan 2.5 to 3.0. Speed and cost vary widely by source: a 14B 720p clip is reported at roughly 1:45 to 20 minutes depending on GPU and distillation, and rented-GPU raw cost is cents per clip against roughly $0.04-0.10 per video second on hosted APIs. Named production use is thin: the only sourced professional example is Chinese event and music-video work, plus hobbyist shorts. Many numbers come from vendor blogs and aggregators and are flagged below. The last section contains inference for your studio, not sourced findings.

## Open weights end at 2.2, so "local Wan" and "hosted Wan" are different products

Wan 2.1 (Feb 2025) and Wan 2.2 (28 Jul 2025) are Apache 2.0. Wan 2.5-Preview, 2.6, 2.7 and 3.0 reportedly have no published weights and run only through Alibaba Cloud Model Studio and resellers; the Wan-AI Hugging Face organisation holds only 2.1/2.2 and derivatives ([HowAIWorks](https://howaiworks.ai/alibaba-wan-open-weights-stopped-at-2-2/)). SEO and affiliate pages contradict this and claim Wan 2.7 open weights ([awesomeagents.ai](https://awesomeagents.ai/models/wan-2-7/)), while another page says a first-party open path was never clearly published ([wan27.org](https://www.wan27.org/blog/wan-2-7-release-date-open-source)). The notes weight the HowAIWorks account higher because it reports checking Hugging Face and GitHub, but nobody verified that directly, so treat it as probable, not settled. The Apache 2.0 licence on Wan 2.2 itself is confirmed on the official repo ([GitHub Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)), and a comparison source notes Wan claims no rights on outputs, whereas LTX-2's community licence requires a paid agreement above $10M revenue ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/ltx-2-3-vs-wan-2-2)).

The version split decides capability. The 2.2 family is T2V-A14B and I2V-A14B (mixture-of-experts, 480P/720P), the dense TI2V-5B (720P at 24 fps, 1280x704), S2V-14B (Aug 2025) and Animate-14B (Sep 2025) ([GitHub Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)). Hosted Wan 2.6 adds reference-to-video, multi-shot from one prompt, native audio with lip sync, 5-15 s clips and 1080p at 24 fps ([MindStudio](https://mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-wan-2-6-video-open-source), a marketing explainer). One source also lists open derivatives on the 2.2 base, Wan-Animate-2 and Wan-Dancer-14B, but it is the only source and unverified ([HowAIWorks](https://howaiworks.ai/alibaba-wan-open-weights-stopped-at-2-2/)).

## Producers pick a variant per job and chain it into a longer cut

The A14B design uses two roughly 14B experts, a high-noise expert for early layout and a low-noise expert for late detail, switched at an SNR threshold, so 14B parameters are active per step out of 27B total ([GitHub Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)). In ComfyUI this means two model loaders and two sampler passes. The speed recipe is the 4-step LightX2V distill, shipped as separate high and low LoRAs or as full distilled checkpoints in BF16 (~28.6 GB), FP8 scaled (~15 GB) or INT8; the high-noise files are described as "more creative", the low-noise as "more stable", and the distills hold only DiT weights, so T5, CLIP vision and VAE are still needed ([lightx2v/Wan2.2-Distill-Models](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Wan2.2-Distill-Models)). Guides split steps at the halfway point, 4 of 8 for example, with Euler or LCM and LoRA strength 1.0 ([RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-workflows/wan-2-2-lightning-t2v-i2v-comfyui-ultra-fast-video-gen)); step counts conflict across pages, so verify in the downloaded workflow. Kijai's WanVideoWrapper is the other stack: fp8 scaled models, GGUF, block swap, and early support for VACE, Animate, MultiTalk, HuMo and camera-control research models, though its own README calls it a personal work-in-progress sandbox ([Kijai/ComfyUI-WanVideoWrapper](https://github.com/kijai/ComfyUI-WanVideoWrapper)). No source benchmarks native against the wrapper.

Control modes are separate models, not one graph. VACE handles reference, masking, extension and clip joining by regenerating a band of frames at each seam, and it also works on non-Wan footage ([VACE Clip Joiner](https://civitai.com/models/2024299/wan-vace-clip-joiner-smooth-ai-video-transitions-for-wan-ltx-2-hunyuan-and-any-other-video-source)). Fun Control and Fun Camera add pose, depth, canny, trajectory and camera paths ([ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/wan/wan2-2-fun-camera)). Animate has a Move mode (a reference character follows a source video's motion) and a Mix/replacement mode, using SAM2 masking and DWPose ([ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/wan/wan2-2-animate)); vendors pitch it for reusing one character across TikTok, Reels and Shorts, but no independent viral example was found ([CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/resource/wan-animate)). S2V-14B takes audio plus a reference image and runs as long as the audio ([ComfyUI S2V docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/video/wan/wan2-2-s2v)); InfiniteTalk, built on Wan 2.1 by another team, offers unlimited length but shows colour shifts past about a minute ([Banodoco KB](https://nathanshipley.github.io/banodoco-kb/kb/wan/index.html), an AI-generated Discord summary). Nobody compared the lip-sync options head to head.

Long video is the hard problem. Reusing the last frame repeatedly accumulates colour drift, "cartoonify" effects and visible cuts ([HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/1st-movie-clip/175306)). Identical first and last frames in loop workflows can degrade output, so a slightly varied end frame is advised ([longform.social](https://longform.social/post/did:plc:pgryn3ephfd2xgft23qokfzt/3mohv4n6alsv2), secondary analysis). The community fix is Stable Video Infinity (SVI), which fine-tunes with error recycling so the model learns to correct its own accumulated mistakes ([arXiv 2510.09212](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.09212v1)). SVI 2.0 Pro added Wan 2.2 A14B support with high and low LoRAs from a single start image ([ComfyUI Wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2025-12-27-svi-2-0-pro-wan-2-2-release)). Tutorial settings are CFG 1.5, shift 8, 6 steps; raising shift from 5 to 12, rendering at 1080p and colour-correcting against the reference reduced drift, and V2 Pro LoRAs reduced repetition ([stablediffusiontutorials](https://www.stablediffusiontutorials.com/2026/01/wan22svi-20-pro.html?m=1), [lilys.ai](https://lilys.ai/ms/notes/comfyui-20260202/comfyui-wan-svi-pro-long-video-no-color-shift)). The paper's benchmarks reach 250 s, so "infinite" exceeds what was tested, and no source compares SVI motion quality with plain chaining.

Two practitioner details matter in daily use. The default ComfyUI I2V workflow ships LightX2V LoRAs that tend to produce slow motion; community "motion fix" LoRAs at strength 1.0 on both experts are offered as a replacement, though that is the author's own impression ([rzgar](https://huggingface.co/rzgar/Wan2.2_I2V_LightX2V_Slow-Mo_Fix)). And negative prompts often do nothing at CFG 1, the distill setting ([HF forum](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/how-to-get-the-most-out-of-prompts-for-wan-models/170354)). Prompts work best as subject/motion, camera, environment, then pace, with explicit camera verbs such as "dolly in" ([Dreamina](https://dreamina.capcut.com/ai-video/wan-2-2-prompt)). For finishing, Wan 2.2 outputs 16 fps, so workflows interpolate with RIFE (integer multipliers only in at least one workflow) and upscale once at the end, bypassing in-graph interpolation ([SeedVR2 workflow](https://prompthero.com/ai-models/seedvr2-1080p-video-upscale-interpolation-comfyui-workflow-download), [comfy.icu](https://comfy.icu/models/2236782/WAN-2-2-1080p-on-Low-VRAM-up-to-4k)). Upscaler opinions conflict: one author prefers TensorRT upscaling over SeedVR2 and FlashVSR, and another reports SeedVR2 taking nearly an hour on an 8 GB card.

## Real-world use is indie shorts and Chinese event work, with few named examples

Verified named use is sparse. At a Wan 2.2 creator event, Li Sanshui of W's Wild Dogs tied Wan 2.2 to the AI music video "Through the Mountains" with NetEase Cloud Music and an AI Spring Festival Gala project, with a 100,000-yuan prize pool, but the article is promotional and does not say which variant or workflow was used ([36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3437960761298561)). The best-documented independent example is a hobbyist's 14-scene anime short made locally with TI2V-5B at 1280x704: 10 variants per scene (130 clips), best picks concatenated with FFmpeg, a multimodal LLM reviewing thumbnail grids, and character consistency across scenes as the main difficulty ([Yakubov](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://yakubov.org/blogs/2025-08-09-ai-video-generation)). Vendors list social creators, ad testing, e-commerce product animation and previs as audiences, with no named customers ([MindStudio](https://mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-wan-2-6-video-open-source)). Reddit, Civitai, Banodoco and YouTube were not reachable, so channel-level and agency evidence is absent; the footprint looks like clips, motion transfer, lip sync and LoRA characters that creators finish in an editor, not single-shot films.

People pick local Wan 2.2 over rivals for licensing, LoRA and control ecosystem, and motion stability; they pick hosted competitors for audio, length and polish. LTX is much faster (hosted 5 s 720p: Wan 232-247 s against LTX-2.3 27-43 s; local 4090: about 4m20s against about 1m30s) ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/ltx-2-3-vs-wan-2-2)), and a Vast.ai comparison calls Wan best for cinematic, controlled output and LTX-2 best for fast iteration with synced audio up to about 20 s ([Vast.ai](https://vast.ai/article/wan-2-2-vs-ltx-2-which-ai-video-model-should-you-use)). Face identity claims conflict between vendor blogs. Aggregators rate Veo 3.1 above Wan 2.6 at roughly 3x the price ($2.00 against $0.75 per clip) ([Gradually](https://www.gradually.ai/en/ai-video-model-comparison/veo-3.1-vs-wan-2.6/)), a commercially motivated source. No credible Runway or Sora head-to-head was found.

## Speed varies fivefold between sources, and rented GPUs cost cents per raw clip

Official guidance says TI2V-5B runs on a 24 GB card and produces 5 s 720p in under 9 minutes on an unnamed consumer GPU, while the 14B models officially need 80 GB single-GPU, with offload flags for smaller cards ([HF Wan2.2-TI2V-5B](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-TI2V-5B), [GitHub Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)). Quantisation closes the gap: the 14B transformer is about 14 GB in FP8, about 28 GB in FP16 and 8-8.5 GB in GGUF Q4/Q5, with the T5-XXL encoder offloadable to RAM ([dev.to GPU guide](https://dev.to/jovan_chan_9500711396d4e6/wan-21-22-and-27-for-local-ai-video-generation-which-gpu-can-actually-run-it-2026-guide-12ka)). Published times for a 5 s 14B 720p clip include about 2:40 on a 4090 and about 1:45 on a 5090 ([dev.to](https://dev.to/thurmon_demich/rtx-5090-vs-4090-for-video-gen-in-2026-32gb-matters-k3i)), 3-5 minutes on A100/H100 at FP16 ([dev.to](https://dev.to/thurmon_demich/best-gpu-for-wan-22-in-2026-5-picks-ranked-14b-ready-120l)), and about 1240 s on a 5090 at 40 default steps ([ac4k mirror](https://github.com/ac4k/Wan2.2)). Those settings differ, none is a controlled benchmark, and no source measured an L40S or a 4090 with fp8 plus Lightning. The SaladCloud Wan 2.1 figures, which predate the quantised workflows, show how steep the scaling is: H100 SXM 85 s at 480p and 284 s at 720p, A100 SXM 170 s and 523 s, and a 4090 281 s at 480p but out of memory at 720p ([dev.to guide quoting Salad](https://dev.to/jovan_chan_9500711396d4e6/wan-21-22-and-27-for-local-ai-video-generation-which-gpu-can-actually-run-it-2026-guide-12ka)). Distillation is the main lever: Lightning claims roughly 20x from 4 steps without CFG ([HF lightx2v](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Wan2.2-Lightning)), and a 2-step community LoRA cut a clip from about 89 s to about 37 s with some loss of fine detail ([Leon1000](https://huggingface.co/Leon1000/Wan2.2_I2V_LightX2V_2Step)).

Hosted prices cluster at about $0.04-0.10 per video second for A14B. fal lists I2V at $0.04, $0.06 and $0.08 per second at 480, 580 and 720p, the 5B fast model at $0.0125-0.025 per video, and speech-to-video at $0.10-0.20 per second ([fal I2V](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/wan/v2.2-a14b/image-to-video/llms.txt), [fal 5B](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/wan/v2.2-5b/text-to-video/fast-wan/llms.txt), [fal S2V](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/wan/v2.2-14b/speech-to-video)). Other figures conflict (a fal learn page says $0.1/s; an aggregator lists turbo variants at $0.05-0.10 per video), so check live pages ([aiidelist](https://aiidelist.com/blog/cheapest-wan-2-2-api-pricing-2026)). Alibaba's own listings are partial and the currency is unclear. For comparison, Veo 3.1 Fast is $0.10-0.12 per second at 720p/1080p and LTX-2.3 Fast about $0.04 per second ([DigitalApplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-video-cost-per-finished-second-reference)).

On rented GPUs, Runpod pods (fetched 22 Nov 2026) list 4090 at $0.89 secure and $0.34 community per hour, 5090 at $1.39 and $0.69, L40S at $1.09 and $0.79, A100 80GB PCIe at $2.09 and $1.19, and H100 SXM at $4.39 and $2.69; serverless runs higher (4090 $1.34, L40S $1.89, H100 $5.89) ([Runpod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)). At those rates a 4-minute clip costs about $0.02 on a community 4090 and about $0.06 secure, before retries, model load and idle time. One tracker assumes 3 attempts per keeper as an illustration only ([DigitalApplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-video-cost-per-finished-second-reference)), and the hobbyist above generated 10 variants per scene. Modal, Lambda, Vast and Together rates were not retrieved.

## Inference for your studio: what to try next and what to avoid

This section is my inference from the sources above and your own prior results, not sourced findings. Your fp8 acceptance matches the roughly 14 GB fp8 footprint, so quality and VRAM are not the open questions; throughput and consistency are.

To try: first, measure your own seconds-per-keeper on the 4090 and the L40S with 4-step distills at 480p and 720p, because no source has a trustworthy figure and published numbers span 5-10x. Second, A/B the "motion fix" LoRAs against your current LightX2V setting if clips look slow, and test 2-step LightX2V only as an off-by-default saving, consistent with your quality-first rule. Third, run the SVI settings that the notes report as fixing colour drift (higher shift, colour-matching each chained clip to the original FLUX.2 keyframe) as a controlled test, since you already run SVI 2.0 but the drift fixes are community-reported only. Fourth, apply interpolation and upscaling once to the assembled edit, not per clip, and bring RIFE to 24 fps via a 2x pass plus retime unless you find a fractional node. Fifth, for a recurring on-screen character, test S2V or Animate for dialogue and character reuse, and VACE joining for seams. Sixth, keep reusing the keyframe as the consistency anchor; train character LoRAs only for recurring characters, per expert, since sources disagree on whether the high-noise expert matters for identity.

To avoid: do not plan around Wan 2.5-3.0 features (native audio, 15 s, 1080p) on your own pods, since the weights are probably unreleased and the contradicting claims are SEO pages. Do not treat vendor and aggregator prices or speed numbers as budget inputs without a live check. Do not use identical first and last frames for loops. Do not mix Wan 2.2 LoRAs into Animate, which the official repo advises against. Do not expect negative prompts to work at CFG 1. Treat Banodoco-derived specifics (for example "40 min for VACE 14B at 720p on a 4090") as unverified. Finally, the pod auto-shutdown and spend-cap guardrails you already have matter more than hourly-rate shopping, because idle burn and retries dominate cost per keeper.

## Conclusion

The sourced evidence shows Wan 2.2 as a toolkit that wins on control, licence and ecosystem and loses on speed, native audio and length, and no verified studio or channel case study shows it carrying a finished film alone. The practical frontier is not another model version but the chaining and finishing layer: drift control, one-pass upscaling and interpolation, and joining. That is where your studio's existing SVI and keyframe discipline already points.

The largest uncertainty is missing evidence, not conflicting evidence. Reddit, Civitai, YouTube and Banodoco were inaccessible, so community quality claims rest on secondary summaries, and there is no controlled benchmark across 4090, 5090, L40S, A100 and H100 with distilled fp8 workflows. A short direct browser pass over those communities, plus your own timing runs, would close most of the gaps.
