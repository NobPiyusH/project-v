# Stop paying for cold starts and redos

*Optimisation review of Project V, 19 Nov 2026. It covers the Science documentary pipeline, the Liminal Drift (Dreamscapes) Shorts pipeline and the daily autopilot. Sources: read-only queries on the studio's own Postgres ledger (`projectv`, `projectv_dream`, `projectv_autopilot`), repo files, official price pages (Modal, RunPod, Google, Anthropic, YouTube Help), and leaderboard, press and vendor data, flagged where weaker. Rupee figures use ₹94 per US$ unless they come from the ledger, which books at ₹88.*

**Bottom line:** Gendu, the engine is already efficient where it counts. A Dream Short's real pictures, clips and writing cost only about **₹15–20**, yet the four finished Shorts cost **₹34–52** each, and the autopilot demo Short cost **₹92.78**. The difference is waste, and nearly all of it comes from two habits. The first is **paying a fresh GPU start-up for every redo round** (58% of the autopilot Short). The second is **redo loops that repeat the same fix without converging**. On the long-form side, **SeedVR2 sharpening is a third of all GPU spend**, and on a 10-minute film it would cost about ₹3,570 on Modal against about ₹255 on a rented RTX 4090. All three fixes keep the same models and the same output, so they pass your "quality first" rule without an A/B test. Two silent faults need attention before any saving. **The Dream quality check fails on every call**, so one of your quality gates is switched off without telling anyone. And **the control plane still listens on the whole network**. On "top 10 in the world": your workflow design (keyframes first, critic, learning loop, claim ledger) already matches what the leading studios and research systems publish. The gap is in the models you feed it. **Z-Image ranks #81 among image models, Chatterbox ranks #86 among voices, and Wan 2.2 is no longer near the video frontier.** Closing that gap is where the money you save should go, but only through A/B tests you judge by eye and ear.

## Start-ups and redo loops eat over half of a Short's budget

Your own ledger shows where the money goes. Between 16 and 19 Nov the studio booked about **₹1,470 of unique spend**: ₹1,196 on long-form and tests, ₹181 on four Dream Shorts, and ₹93 on the one autopilot demo. Modal H100 time is **65% of the main ledger** (cost ledger, `cost_entry` grouped by provider). On Dream Shorts, **Wan start-up and model load is the single largest line at ₹81.09, 45% of all Dream spend**. The clips themselves cost ₹62.71. None of the four Shorts met your ₹20 target ([dream-shorts plan](../docs/plans/dream-shorts.md)).

The autopilot Short shows the mechanism most clearly. It booked **four start-ups at ₹13.52 each (₹54.08, 58% of the Short)** against just ₹19.56 for 13 clips, which works out to about ₹1.6 a clip at 480p. The Producer redid pictures three times and clips three times, then asked you on round four anyway, because the problem ("two shots contain visible human figures") was the same every round (`producer_decision` rows, `projectv_autopilot`). Earlier commits had already tried to fix people appearing through prompt wording. That tells you this is a prompt and model problem, not a dice-roll problem, and more seeds won't fix it. A "same issue twice, ask the owner" rule, plus a firm negative prompt, saves most of that spend. It also saves your time, because the question reaches you after one round rather than four.

The start-up waste is a design choice you can undo cheaply. All the Modal apps use a 30 s `scaledown_window` ([wan.py](../workers/modal_apps/wan.py)). A 2-minute warm window was added and then removed on the same day, on the reasoning that "redos come after the finish". In autopilot mode that reasoning no longer holds: the Producer reviews each batch 18–22 s after it is saved, and redo renders land within about a minute. There is a second wrinkle. **The ledger books a start-up for every container requested, not every cold start observed** (`DreamService.java` lines 704–709, `clips.py` lines 275–285). Real cold start plus load is about **105 s of wall time in the worker log, against the 140 s booked** ([worker log](../logs/worker.err.log)). So the ledger probably over-books some start-ups and, as the next section shows, under-books Gemini. Either way the Producer is deciding on wrong numbers.

The fix keeps the same model and the same output. Keep one container warm across the Producer's review (minutes, not hours; `min_containers` would bill around the clock and breaks rule 8), or queue redo shots into the same container call. The ledger arithmetic says this alone takes the autopilot Short from **₹92.78 to about ₹45–50**. Batching several Shorts per start, for example rendering tomorrow's autopilot clips alongside today's, cuts it further. Modal's GPU memory snapshots are a second option, and you already have a test app (`projectv-wan-snap`). But they are **alpha**, they **don't speed up reading weights from storage**, and every redeploy invalidates them ([Modal docs](https://modal.com/docs/guide/memory-snapshot)). Measure them before counting on them.

## Upscaling, not generation, is the long-form money pit

On long-form, **SeedVR2 sharpening cost ₹391.82 across three runs, 33% of the main ledger**, at ₹8.5, ₹5.5 and ₹11.9 per clip. The smallest batch (7 clips) was the dearest per clip, because each run pays its own start-up on a single H100 container (cost ledger, `SHARPEN` rows). The hero keyframes show a related pattern: one video used **24 Nano Banana images at about ₹3.1 each**, against about ₹0.2 for a Z-Image frame, which points to repeated paid redos before the shot list was locked.

Scale this to a 10-minute film and it becomes the biggest decision in the pipeline. A 10-minute film at 24 fps is 14,400 frames. SeedVR2 3B fp8 runs at about 2.4 s per frame on an H100 and about 2.0 s on an RTX 4090 ([numz SeedVR2 README](https://huggingface.co/numz/SeedVR2_comfyUI/blob/b77d5dd95dba3f25e3a28064b76e6028a5152368/README.md)). At Modal's **$3.95/h for an H100** that is about **$38 (₹3,570) to reach 1080p**. A RunPod Community 4090 costs **$0.34/h**, so the same job is about **$2.7 (₹255)** ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing), [RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)). A 4K master multiplies both by roughly 3–4. That is the difference between a 4K master costing about ₹10,000+ or about ₹750. Upscaling is a long, steady batch job, which is exactly what cheap pods suit. Generation, by contrast, comes in short bursts, where Modal's per-second billing and scale-to-zero earn their higher price. Wan 14B should stay on Modal H100s: an L40S would likely cost the same per clip and run slower, and RunPod's serverless H100 is actually *dearer* than Modal at $5.89/h.

Two cautions. First, moving to a pod spends money on a new account and creates a machine that can be left running, so it is **your decision**, and the watchdog has to come with it (rule 8). Second, interpolating to 24 fps before upscaling means 50% more frames to upscale. Upscaling at 16 fps and interpolating afterwards would be cheaper, but nobody has shown that it looks the same, so that change is **off until A/B-tested**.

## What a Short and a film should really cost

Here are realistic targets, built from list prices and your measured timings. They are not end-to-end measurements yet.

| Item | Liminal Drift Short (≈30 s, ≈10 clips) | 10-min documentary, Wan fast | 10-min documentary, hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wan clips | ≈₹16–19 at 480p | ≈₹1,930 (156 clips × ₹12.4) | ≈₹7,800 (156 × ₹50) |
| Start-ups | ≈₹8 once (≈₹0 when batched across Shorts) | ≈₹65 (8 containers) | ≈₹65 |
| Keyframes | ₹1–3 (Z-Image) | ≈₹250 (Nano Banana 2.1 batch) | ≈₹250 |
| Upscale to 1080p | none | ≈₹255 on a 4090 pod (≈₹3,570 on Modal) | ≈₹255 |
| Music, voice, LLM | ≈₹6–13 | ≈₹90–210 | ≈₹90–210 |
| **Total** | **≈₹27–43** (≈₹20 with 4+ Shorts per start) | **≈₹2,600–2,700** | **≈₹8,500** |
| Wall time | ≈10–20 min on one container | ≈40 min of clips on 8 containers, plus upscale | ≈2.6 h of clips on 8 containers |

Unit prices come from [Modal](https://modal.com/pricing), the [Gemini API pricing page](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing) and [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing). Clip timings are your own benchmarks: fast is about 115–120 s per 720p clip, hybrid about 483 s, standard about 1,141 s, and the model load is 77 s ([SESSION-HANDOFF](../docs/SESSION-HANDOFF.md)). These figures replace the ₹1,100–1,700 film estimate in the 18 Nov review, which left out realistic take counts and upscaling. One number needs settling before you plan a hybrid film. An earlier repo report put hybrid at about ₹25 per clip, but 483 s on an H100 implies about ₹50. That gap swings the film total by about ₹4,000, so re-measure it from `cost_entry` on the next run.

Your ₹20 Short is reachable without touching quality, but only by batching four or more Shorts per GPU start, which fits a daily autopilot well. Getting there by dropping resolution is a different matter. Wan draft-class 480p is what the current Shorts already use. Whether it holds up on a phone next to channels rendering 720p+ is your eye's call, so treat it as **off until A/B-tested** as a permanent standard.

Turnaround is not your bottleneck. The autopilot Short took about **62 minutes**, mostly in redo rounds. Once the stop rule and warm redos are in, expect about 20–30 minutes. Long-form stage medians are short too (Script 14 s, Music 38 s, Narration 97 s). The long tails come from review waits and repeated attempts: Animatic re-ran 14 times, up to attempt 9, and Assembly 8 times (`stage_run`). Faster models won't fix that. Catching problems earlier will, and the cheapest catch is approving a keyframe before you pay to animate it.

## The ledger undercounts Gemini and over-books the GPU

Your config records that **Google's real bill ran about 1.53× the ledger's estimates** ([providers.yaml](../config/providers.yaml)). The cap uses that calibration, but the router's cost term does not. So the λ the router uses to trade quality against cost is computed on Gemini prices that are too low. On top of that, 90% of ledger rows are estimates, and `amount_usd` is NULL on every row. Modal's real billing is fetched every 10 minutes by `modal_billing.py`, but it only shows on the dashboard and never reaches `cost_entry`. The FX rate in config is ₹88, while the rupee traded at about **₹93.6–94 per dollar this week** ([HDFC Sky/PTI](https://hdfcsky.com/news/rupee-rises-31-paise-to-93-63-against-us-dollar-in-early-trade-november-17-2026)), so every dollar line reads about 7% low.

The scheduled price rise makes this more urgent. **Gemini 3.6/3.8 Flash text goes from $0.75/$3.75 to $1.50/$7.50 per million tokens on 1 Jan 2027, and Flash TTS output from $9 to $18 per million audio tokens.** The Gemini 2.5 models are rerouted on 1 Dec 2026 ([Gemini API pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). LLM spend is small for you (Sonnet scripts cost rupees, not hundreds), so the lever here is accuracy rather than savings. Caching your style bible and channel lessons gives cache hits at 0.05× the input price on Sonnet 5.5, and the Batch API halves the rest ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Both are worth doing because they are free, but they save tens of rupees per film, not thousands. Only 15 routing decisions exist against 284 cost rows, so most paid calls (Producer reviews, Scout, TTS) never pass through the router at all. That is a rule 6 gap as well as a cost-visibility gap.

## Three silent faults outrank every saving

**The Dream quality check fails on every call.** Gemini's Developer API rejects the `additionalProperties` that a `dict[str, int]` field produces. The gemini_pool classifies the error as "other" with a 0 s cooldown, so it fails quietly instead of failing over ([worker log](../logs/worker.err.log), [check.py](../workers/dream/check.py)). Shorts are passing a gate that isn't running. This is **CRITICAL** because it touches quality, and the fix takes an hour.

**The control plane listens on 0.0.0.0:8080 with no login**, while every Docker service and the dashboard are on 127.0.0.1. Your own autopilot doc says nobody else on the Wi-Fi can reach the studio, which isn't true for the API ([application.properties](../control-plane/src/main/resources/application.properties), [autopilot.md](../docs/autopilot.md)). The 18 Nov review flagged this too. It is a one-line fix and remains **HIGH**.

**The money code is untested on `main`.** The `ledger` package (Pricing, BudgetGuard, CostLedger) has 391 lines and no tests. The cap tests and the `start.ps1` hang fix exist only on unmerged commit `083de25`. Backups are a manual script that has run twice, both on 19 Nov. It writes to the same C: drive and skips SeaweedFS media and Temporal history ([backup.ps1](../scripts/backup.ps1)). That last item is **MEDIUM**, but it is cheap to fix.

## Getting to "top 10" is a model question, not an engineering one

The published playbook of serious AI studios is research first, a locked look through keyframes, I2V in 3–8 s chunks, premium narration, and an edited finish ([shortsparks](https://shortsparks.com/how-to-make-youtube-shorts-with-ai/), [boldlywithai](https://www.boldlywithai.com/blog/ai-filmmaking-workflow-2026)). Your pipeline already follows it. Current research adds **best-of-k candidates scored by a VLM critic, with targeted per-shot regeneration and prompt memory** ([ViMax](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07649v1), [VQQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12310)). Your picture check, Producer and learning loop are an early version of exactly that. Once the check works again, the natural upgrade is two or three candidates per shot, scored by the critic, with the same-issue stop rule on top. Your Science roadmap's "claim ledger first" plan also matches top-tier practice: Kurzgesagt spends about **100 hours of human fact-checking per video** ([Kurzgesagt summary](https://recapio.com/digest/ai-slop-is-killing-our-channel-by-kurzgesagt-in-a-nutshell)).

The models are where you trail. On Artificial Analysis's image arena, **Z-Image Turbo sits at #81 (Elo 937), while Nano Banana 2.1 is #8 (Elo 1176) at $0.0336 an image** ([AA text-to-image](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)). Your earlier side-by-side picked Z-Image for Dream pictures, and that taste call stands. But a 240-point gap means the Science keyframes deserve a fresh test. For video, the image-to-video leaders are **Seedance 2.5 (#1) and MiniMax H3 768p (#4, open weights, $4.80/min)**. Wan 2.2's open line stopped at 2.2, and Veo 3.1, Kling and Runway are not in the I2V top 15 ([AA image-to-video](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). For voice, **Chatterbox open-source ranks #86, against Eleven v4 at #1, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS at #6 and Fish Audio Drama 3 (open) at #7** ([AA TTS](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)). That matches your own unhappiness with the narration. You asked to pause voice tests on 18 Nov, so these are candidates for whenever you choose to resume, not a request to resume now. Arena rankings are averages of strangers' taste and many confidence intervals overlap, so they tell you what to test, not what to pick. Your ear and eye decide.

The channel side rewards the same direction. YouTube allows AI and monetises it. What it removes is content that "looks like it's made with a template" and "unrelated or inconsistent AI clips" stitched "to surprise or shock" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). A music-only liminal channel sits close to both lines. Your defences are the named series, the recurring hero object, a deliberate camera and your rating loop. Keep upload volume below what you can personally review, because enforcement reportedly looks across about the last 30 uploads ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)). Two practical rules follow. A Short over 60 s with **any** Content ID claim is blocked worldwide, so never register a generated track with a distributor ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)). And photoreal imagery is now auto-labelled, a label YouTube says carries no reach penalty, so disclosing yourself costs nothing ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/youtube-automatic-ai-labels-disclosure-may-2026)). The money sits in long-form. Education & Science has the highest median RPM, about **$10.22**, while Shorts earn about 3–14% of a channel's long-form RPM ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels), vendor data). So judge Liminal Drift on subscribers and `engagedViews`, not revenue ([YouTube Reporting API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/reporting/revision_history)). Once uploads begin, feed the retention curve back to shot IDs through the learning loop you already have.

## Conclusion

The surprising finding is how little of the studio's spend buys picture. At 480p a Wan clip costs about ₹1.6. The rest of each Short goes on idle-to-warm transitions and loops that ask the same question four times. So the efficiency work is mostly about **when** the studio spends (one warm GPU per batch, upscaling only on locked shots, cheap pods for long batch jobs) and **when it stops** (same issue twice means ask Gendu). That work frees about half of a Short's budget and about ₹3,300 per film, at no cost to quality.

That freed budget is what should pay for "top 10". The engineering is already world-class in shape. The output will reach that level only when the keyframe, video and voice models catch up with the workflow, and only you can judge those swaps in A/B tests. Treat every figure here as provisional until the ledger is calibrated against Google's and Modal's real bills. Today it undercounts one and over-books the other, so the cheapest, highest-value fix of all may be making the numbers honest before trusting any of them.
