# Short: Lake Natron, "The lake that turns animals to stone?" (spooky cut)

Vertical 9:16, about 52 s. Channel: Strange If True. Tone: low, slow, whispered; spooky first, then a warm twist. Every claim below was checked on 23 Nov 2026 (sources at the end). Nothing here says the lake kills animals or turns them to stone.

## Voiceover (about 120 words)
1. In Tanzania there is a lake the colour of rust and blood.
2. Its water is fiercely alkaline. Some animals that come near it end up on its shore, pale and perfectly still.
3. Photographs of them went around the world: birds and bats, frozen in stone.
4. So the lake turns animals to stone?
5. No. Those animals were already dead.
6. Salt minerals from the water dried over their bodies. They were not turned to stone. They were coated, and preserved.
7. Then a photographer found them... and arranged them for the camera.
8. So the statues are real. The curse is not.
9. And here is the strangest part. This same lake is a nursery. Most of the world's lesser flamingos hatch here.
10. Death on the shore. Life on the water.
11. Strange... if true.

End card: "Which strange story should we check next?"

## Facts and sources (checked 23 Nov 2026)
- Brandt found dead animals on the shore and posed them (Across the Ravaged Land, 2013): PetaPixel, National Geographic.
- Geologist Thure Cerling (National Geographic): probably not truly calcified, coated with sodium carbonate or bicarbonate; the animals likely died naturally, perhaps confused by the sky's reflection. So say "coated" and "preserved", never "calcified".
- About 75% of the world's lesser flamingos hatch here; the only regular lesser-flamingo breeding site in Africa: Smithsonian, Live Science, WWF.
- pH: sources range from 9 to 10.5 (NASA) up to over 12. Do not state one number.
